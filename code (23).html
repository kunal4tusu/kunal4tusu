Indian Economy ~ Eve of Independence

Revision Notes: 1947 & Before

1. Introduction & Context

  • British Rule's Purpose: Primarily to make India a supplier of raw materials and a consumer market for Britain's industries.
  • Nature: Exploitative, leading to fundamental (often detrimental) changes in the Indian economy's structure.
  • Importance: Understanding the pre-1947 state is crucial to grasp post-independence challenges and development paths.

2. Low Economic Development

  • Pre-British Era: Independent economy, known for agriculture and vibrant handicrafts (cotton/silk textiles like Daccai Muslin/Malmal, metal/stone works).
  • Under Colonial Rule: Became stagnant and underdeveloped.
  • National Income: No sincere official estimates. Notable attempts by Dadabhai Naoroji, William Digby, Findlay Shirras, V.K.R.V. Rao (most significant), R.C. Desai.
  • Growth: Aggregate real output grew < 2%; Per capita output grew meagerly ~0.5% (first half 20th C).

3. Agricultural Sector

  • Dominance: Main livelihood for ~85% of the population.
  • Condition: Marked by stagnation and low productivity, despite some expansion of cultivated area.
  • Causes of Stagnation:
    • Land Settlement Systems: Especially the Zamindari System (Bengal Presidency), where Zamindars focused on rent collection (often ruthlessly due to fixed deposit dates) not improvement.
    • Technology: Low levels used.
    • Infrastructure: Lack of irrigation, negligible fertiliser use.
    • Commercialisation: Shift to cash crops benefitted Britain, often harmed farmer self-sufficiency & food security.
    • Investment: Sector starved of funds for improvements (terracing, flood control, drainage).

4. Industrial Sector

  • De-industrialisation: Systematic decline of India's famed handicraft industries.
  • Colonial Motives: Secure raw materials for Britain; Create a captive market for British goods.
  • Impact: Massive unemployment; Dependence on imported goods.
  • Modern Industry Growth (Post 1850s): Very slow.
    • Early Mills: Cotton (West India, Indian dominated) & Jute (Bengal, Foreigner dominated).
    • Later Developments: Iron & Steel (TISCO est. 1907); Limited sugar, cement, paper post-WWII.
  • Key Weaknesses:
    • Absence of Capital Goods Industry (machine-making).
    • Very limited Public Sector role (mostly railways, ports, power).
    • Low contribution to GDP.

5. Foreign Trade

  • Structure Shift: India became exporter of primary products (raw materials like cotton, jute, indigo) & importer of finished goods (textiles, machinery) from Britain.
  • Control: British Monopoly over trade direction; >50% trade with Britain. Limited trade elsewhere (China, Ceylon, Persia).
  • Suez Canal (1869): Intensified British control by reducing transport time/cost.
  • Export Surplus Paradox: Large surplus existed, but caused scarcity of essentials domestically and led to the "Drain of Wealth" (funded British expenses, not Indian development). No inflow of gold/silver.

6. Demographic Condition

  • Census: First official census in 1881; held every 10 years.
  • Transition Stage: Year 1921 known as the "Year of Great Divide" marking shift to second stage (though high growth came later).
  • Poor Social Indicators:
    • Low Literacy (< 16% overall; ~7% female).
    • Poor Public Health (facilities inadequate/absent).
    • High prevalence of water/air-borne diseases.
    • Very High Mortality: Overall & Infant Mortality Rate (~218 per 1000).
    • Low Life Expectancy (~32 years).
    • Widespread Poverty.

7. Occupational Structure

  • Definition: Distribution of workforce across sectors.
  • Agriculture Dominance: Overwhelming share (70-75%).
  • Other Sectors: Manufacturing (~10%), Services (15-20%).
  • Regional Variation: Some shift away from agriculture (Madras Presidency, Bombay, Bengal); Increased dependence in others (Orissa, Rajasthan, Punjab).

8. Infrastructure

  • Colonial Motive: Served colonial interests (resource extraction, army movement, control) over public welfare.
  • Roads: Poor quality; lacked all-weather roads, especially to rural areas.
  • Railways (Intro 1850): Significant but mixed impact:
    • Positive: Enabled long travel, broke some social barriers.
    • Negative: Facilitated resource drain, hurt village self-sufficiency (via commercialisation), economic cost high.
  • Other: Water transport underdeveloped; Telegraph for law & order; Postal services useful but inadequate.

9. Conclusion: The Legacy at Independence

India inherited an economy facing enormous challenges...

  • Agriculture: Burdened by surplus labour & low productivity.
  • Industry: Needed modernisation, diversification, investment.
  • Trade: Skewed towards Britain's needs.
  • Infrastructure: Required massive upgradation & public orientation.
  • Society: Plagued by rampant poverty & unemployment.
